# Landing Page Audit

Perspective: independent restaurant owner deciding whether to request a short review.

## Summary

The page is visually credible and the core idea is relevant, but it still needs more buyer-facing clarity. A restaurant owner needs to know what is being offered, who it is for, what happens during the review, what data is required, and why this is not another software rollout.

## What Looked Like A Student Project

- Earlier language around concepts, pilots, first versions, and feedback made the page feel exploratory instead of service-ready.
- The demo dashboard was useful, but the page needed stronger framing that the numbers are sample data and the service is a practical review.
- The page did not clearly explain what happens after clicking the CTA, which can make the business feel unfinished.

## Trust Reducers

- The page did not clearly state boundaries: no private financial data, no POS replacement, no software migration, and no obligation.
- The founder/local note helped, but the footer was too light for a service page.
- There was not enough explanation of how the service works before asking for contact details.

## Unfinished Signals

- The secondary CTA “Request details” was vague.
- The contact form was asking for input before fully explaining the review.
- There was no “who this is for” section, which matters because restaurants vary by size and workflow maturity.

## Missing Sections

- Who the service is for.
- What the review includes.
- What the review does not require.
- What happens after the restaurant submits the form.
- A concise trust/privacy boundary near the CTA.

## Weak Or Confusing Copy

- “Request details” is less clear than “See what gets checked” or “Book a 10-minute review.”
- “Workflow review” is useful but should be supported by concrete examples.
- “Restaurant Admin Helper” needs service framing throughout so it does not read like only a software name.

## Improvements Implemented

- Strengthened CTAs around “Book a 10-minute review” and “See what gets checked.”
- Added a “Who this is for” section.
- Added a “What the review includes” section.
- Added a trust boundary section covering no private financial data, existing tools, local Toronto/GTA support, and sample/blurred data.
- Tightened contact and footer language so the page feels like a professional cost-control service.

## Remaining Recommendation

After the first real conversations, add honest proof: founder bio, service examples, anonymized before/after workflow screenshots, or a short “what restaurants usually bring to the review” section. Do not add fake testimonials, logos, customers, or metrics.
